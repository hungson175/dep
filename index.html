What changed for DeepSeek?

The original strict run scored 178/231 (77.06%), with 218 valid distributions and 13 missing-candidate failures. The new default returns 0% for unreported options and renormalizes available candidates. Replaying those exact saved responses recovers all 13 maps; 12 become correct and one remains wrong.

All originally valid distributions stay unchanged. Zero-fill is a censored-probability approximation, not a measured zero; it can inflate confidence. All-missing or malformed reports still fail. The strict result and original evidence remain preserved.

Reproduction, not rank claims

  • Bonsai: Ternary-Bonsai-2-27B-PQ2_0.gguf, llama.cpp, unchanged minijev prompts, equal +50 candidate bias, temperature 1, one output token, native candidate probabilities. No zero-fill was needed.
  • DeepSeek: deepseek-flash (V4.1 Flash), unchanged notebook prompts, thinking off, temperature 1, one output token, top-20 logprobs, no logit bias.
  • Original Jev: jev-1.13.0, pinned upstream TypeSafe adapter, unchanged state/instructions/criteria, native returned probabilities. No custom one-token prompting or zero-fill; no distributions required renormalization.
  • Same exact 231 public task IDs, same label scoring. Failed decisions stay in the denominator. Gold answers and provenance are not sent to any model.
  • These runs do not prove adversarial safety, universal reliability, or empirical calibration. They do not benchmark Quyet or establish superiority over current official systems.
